Income and bills that repeat

Salary, rent and subscriptions: set them up once.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Step by step
  • Keeping the list right
  • Found in your history
  • When a bill is due
  • Good to know
  • Related

Income and recurring bills is the list in Plan of everything that repeats: your salary, your rent, your subscriptions. Set each one up once, and the forecast counts it on its schedule.

When to use it

  • You get paid on a regular rhythm and want it counted in the forecast.
  • You have bills that repeat: rent, school, subscriptions.
  • Damla has noticed payments that repeat in your history and is offering them to you.
  • A repeating payment has been recorded or skipped by mistake and you need to undo it.

Step by step

  1. Open the Plan tab and tap Income & bills.
  2. Tap Add (and if nothing is set up yet, the screen offers Add one).
  3. Choose the kind: Bill or Income. The Kind control at the top of the form does this.
  4. Enter a name, the amount, and the account it moves through (Paid from for a bill, Paid into for income).
  5. If the amount changes, turn on Amount varies and enter a lowest and highest amount instead of one figure.
  6. Choose Repeats: Every month, Every week, Every 2 weeks, Every 3 months, Every 6 months, Every year, Custom… for any rhythm (every N days, weeks, months or years), or Once.
  7. If it ends, turn on Stops after a number of payments and set how many. The date field then reads First payment.
  8. For income, choose How sure: Fixed: arrives on time, Varies: use the lowest recent, or Irregular: count only when received.
  9. Tap Save.

Keeping the list right

The list is sorted with what is due soonest first, under Income and Bills sections. Each row shows its name, its rhythm, and how it stands: Due today or Due tomorrow in orange when a bill is here, Overdue in red when it has passed, or the next date. For income the same lines read Expected today or Expected tomorrow.

Found in your history

At the top of the list, Damla shows payments it has noticed recurring in your history with a steady amount and rhythm. Each has an Add button that puts it in your forecast; swipe one away and tap Not a bill if it is not one. Nothing is added from history without your tap.

When a bill is due

  • A bill or income due tomorrow, today or late, and not recorded yet, shows on Today under Due. A line under Coming up opens the item behind it when there is one: a bill or income opens its page, a card bill opens its payment sheet, a staged purchase opens its plan.
  • Open the item to see when it is next due, then adjust the amount, date, account or category if this time is different.
  • Tap Mark paid (or Mark received for income). Damla adds the transaction, marks that one done and moves the next due date on. It says what it recorded and closes the screen.
  • Turn on Use these details from now on to keep a new amount or account for the next times too.
  • Tap Skip this one if it did not come this time: it is marked done without recording anything.
  • You can record early. If the next one is not due for more than a week, Damla asks before recording ("Yes, I paid it early" / "Yes, I received it"), so a second tap does not record next month's by accident.
  • If a payment for that amount is already in your books (from a bank text, say), the bill is treated as paid and no reminder shows.

Good to know

Note: Recorded or skipped one by mistake? Open the item and tap Undo the last one…. It becomes due again, and you can remove the payment that was recorded for it or keep it.

  • How sure matters for the cautious forecast line: Fixed income is counted as you said it, Varies uses the lowest recent amount, and Irregular is only counted once it is actually received. The cautious line delays or discounts income that is not fixed.
  • Swipe a row and tap Delete to remove it; use Edit on its page to change anything about it.
  • The item's page keeps a short Recorded here section: the last payments recorded against it.
  • A loan instalment you mark paid here pays the loan down; it is not counted as spending.
