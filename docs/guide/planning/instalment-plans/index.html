Instalment plans

Spread a card purchase over months and see the effect.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Step by step
  • Telling Damla in a sentence
  • From a card statement
  • How the money is worked out
  • Good to know
  • Related

An instalment plan is a card purchase your bank lets you pay off month by month, kept in Damla as dated payments rather than one large bill. You find the plans in the Plan tab under Instalment plans, and the forecast charges each payment on its due date until the plan is done.

When to use it

  • Your bank has approved turning a card purchase into instalments and gave you the numbers.
  • A statement already shows instalment rows (an "EMI 2 of 6" line and the like), and you want the rest counted.
  • You want to see the instalments end on their own and the card return to normal.
  • A purchase was fully refunded or the bank recall ended the plan.

Step by step

  1. Make sure the purchase is recorded as a card purchase, either by hand or from a bank alert.
  2. Open the Activity tab, find the purchase, swipe it right and tap Turn into instalments.
  3. The screen is titled Turn into instalments, with the purchase and its amount at the top under Purchase. Fill in the numbers As your bank states it: the Monthly instalment, the number of months (2 to 60, with a stepper), a Plan fee in total (optional), and the date of the First instalment.
  4. Tap Convert.
  5. Damla shows the plan in the Plan tab under Instalment plans: what is still to pay, a line like "monthly instalment × months months · N left · card name", and "Costs X extra in total" if there is a fee.

To try a plan without committing it: in the Plan tab, open What if, tap Add a change, pick the Instalments kind, and fill in a name, the Monthly instalment, the months, the first date, and the account it would be paid from. The plan becomes real only when you convert it.

Telling Damla in a sentence

You can also type it. A sentence that mentions instalments, EMI or taksit can bring up the same questions ("What is the monthly instalment?", "How many months?"). Because a plan is a commitment, Damla shows the result under Check and save first: the plan's name, the instalment and months, the first date, and the fee if any. Nothing is written until you tap Create plan, which sits under the note "This creates a commitment, so please check it." If a purchase is already on a plan, Damla says "That purchase is already on instalments."

From a card statement

When an imported card statement has rows that show which payment they are (EMI 2/6, Instalment 3 of 12, taksit 02/06 and the like), the import lists them under Instalments found. Nothing is added until you confirm. The rows are filed under the category Instalments, not as everyday spending, and stay a charge in that statement's bill. The payments still to come join your plan on the card's later due dates. On each later statement, the next number appears, the plan moves forward, and no payment is counted twice. The plan leaves the forecast by itself after the last payment.

How the money is worked out

Use the numbers from the bank's offer. The footer on the form says: "Damla does not guess interest rates." The plan's figures follow from what you state:

  • Each payment in between is the instalment you stated.
  • The last payment is whatever makes all the payments add up exactly to the total.
  • The total is the first of these that applies: a total your bank stated, otherwise the purchase plus the stated fee, otherwise the instalment times the months.
  • The extra cost of the plan is the difference between that total and the purchase.

Damla checks the stated numbers: the term must be at least 1 month, the instalment and the purchase above zero, and all amounts in one currency. If the numbers leave nothing for the last payment, Damla refuses and says why. A stated total that differs a lot from the instalment times the months is kept as a warning on the plan. A plan always needs your OK before it is written, so you can check the numbers there.

Good to know

  • The original purchase stays in your books as the spending it was, on the day it happened. The plan adds each payment on its due date until it ends. Instalment payments are left out of the estimate of a card's typical month, so they are never counted twice.
  • Each payment reaches the forecast as its own dated payment and stops by itself after the last one. A plan taken from a statement runs on the card's own later statement due dates; a plan you set up yourself runs monthly from the First instalment date you gave.
  • Swipe the plan's row and tap Cancel plan when the bank ends or recalls it, for example when the purchase was fully refunded. Payments after that day are no longer counted, and the row shows Cancelled instead of what was left.
  • A plan made from a statement follows the bank's own numbering, so it stays in step even if you never entered it by hand.
  • These figures describe a payment schedule the bank has offered. Whether to take the plan, and what it costs you, is your decision, not advice.
