What if

Try a purchase, a loan or a late payment before you do it.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Step by step
  • A worked example
  • Good to know
  • Related

What if is where you try a change before you make it: spend an amount on a date, receive one, leave a payment out, or take on an instalment plan. Damla compares your forecast with and without the change, so you can see what it would do to your lowest point and your safe to spend before you commit.

When to use it

  • You are thinking of a big one-off purchase and want to know what it does to the weeks around it.
  • You are considering an instalment plan and want to see the monthly payment in your forecast before you agree to it.
  • An income might arrive late, or you might skip a bill, and you want to see what that costs you.
  • Money is expected on a date that is not in your books yet, and you want it counted.

Step by step

  1. Open the Forecast from the Your balance ahead chart on Today, and tap What if at the top right.
  2. Tap Add a change.
  3. Choose the kind at the top: Spend, Receive, Skip or Instalments.
  4. For Spend or Receive: name it under What, enter the Amount, pick the Date, and choose the account it is Paid from or goes Into.
  5. For Skip: choose under Leave out which recurring payment or plan to leave out — an income or bill, an instalment plan, or a staged purchase.
  6. For Instalments: enter the Monthly instalment, choose the number of months (from 2 to 60), pick the First instalment date, and choose the account it is Paid from.
  7. Tap Add. The change joins the list under Changes to try.
  8. Read the Result above it: Lowest point and Safe to spend, each shown as it was, with an arrow to what it becomes with your changes.
  9. Check the warning line: if the changes would take your balance under your minimum, a red warning says by how much and on which date. If not, a green line says the balance stays above your minimum for the next year.
  10. To try another combination, swipe a change to remove it. Tap Done when you are finished.

A worked example

An invented household, on 9 November. Currency AED, minimum balance AED 1,000, looking ahead at least 30 days. One bank account and one salary:

What countsValue
Everyday account (bank)AED 29,600 today
Internet, monthly on the 12thAED 350 out
Rent, monthly on the 16thAED 8,000 out
Salary, monthly on the 27thAED 25,000 in

With no changes, Damla shows a lowest point of AED 21,250 on Mon 16 Nov and a safe to spend of AED 20,250. The question What if answers: what is the most you could pay as one payment on a given date, and still keep the AED 1,000 minimum?

Payment dateMost you could payComfortable amount
10 Nov, 13 Nov or 19 NovAED 20,000AED 16,000
29 Nov (after salary)AED 36,500AED 29,500

How to read it: the same payment has a lower limit before payday and a higher one once the salary on 27 November has landed. What matters is the lowest point the weeks after the payment reach, not the balance today. The savings in this household, AED 40,000, are in none of these figures, because that account is marked not in forecast.

Good to know

Note: What if is only a preview. Nothing on the screen is recorded, and the changes exist only while it is open. Closing it leaves your books exactly as they were.

Limit: The comparison looks a year ahead. If you remove a change and the result does not move, check that the change was added: it is listed under Changes to try.

Tip: The starting numbers come from your forecast, so they are only as good as what is in it: your income and bills, your cards' due dates and your instalment plans. See Forecast and safe to spend.

Note: The numbers are information and estimates from your own books, not financial advice.
