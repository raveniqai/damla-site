Loans

Track a loan, its instalments and what is left to repay.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Step by step
  • Good to know
  • Related

A loan in Damla is the money you still owe, kept as its own account, together with the instalments that pay it off. The instalments go into your forecast until the last one, so your safe-to-spend figure already allows for them.

When to use it

  • You are paying off a loan in instalments — a car loan, a personal loan, a student loan — and want the payments counted in your forecast until the loan is finished.
  • You want to see in one place the instalment amount, how often it falls, how many payments are left and what is still owed.
  • You want to record each instalment as you pay it, including the part that is interest.
  • You want to check a loan's balance against what your bank says you owe.

Step by step

Add a loan

  1. Open Settings, then Set-up guide. Tick Loans and debts, and tap Start. On the step, tap Add, then A loan with instalments.
  2. You can also type a sentence in the box under Or just type it instead of filling the form. Either way, Damla shows you the loan before anything is saved.
  3. Enter the details: who lent it, in Lender (for example the bank); the amount still to repay, in Still to repay; the amount of the Instalment; how often it falls — every month, every 2 months, or any every-N days, weeks, months or years; how many instalments are left, in Instalments left; the date of the Next instalment; and the account that pays, in Paid from.
  4. Tap Save.
  5. Open Accounts and check that the loan is listed under Loans, as money you owe.

The instalments are in your forecast from then on, until the last one, and then they stop.

Record an instalment

  1. Open the loan from Accounts and tap the card to flip it.
  2. Tap Details.
  3. Under Record an instalment, choose the account the instalment was paid from (Paid from). Enter the amount paid and, if your bank states it, how much of it was interest (Of which interest (optional)).
  4. Check the date in Paid on — it can be today or earlier — then tap Record the instalment.

The loan goes down by the part that is not interest. The interest is booked as a cost, under Fees & interest. Damla does not calculate interest; it uses the numbers from your bank.

Good to know

  • The back of the loan card shows what matters for this kind of account: the instalment, how often it falls, how many instalments are left, the next instalment, the date of the last one (when the loan ends), the account that pays it and what is still owed. If no instalment plan is set up for the loan, the card says so.
  • Use the set-up guide for a loan you are actively paying off. Adding a loan from Accounts with the plus button, then Add an account, then a Type of Loan, records only the debt itself. Without an instalment, it is not in your forecast.
  • A loan you take out to buy a car or a property is created together with the purchase (see Property and cars).
  • A loan instalment you mark paid from Today or from Plan pays the loan down. It is not counted as spending.
  • Over time a loan balance can drift from your bank's figure. Correct it to the balance your bank shows now and then. The correction shows as an adjustment, which is neither income nor spending.
  • The Loans section on the Accounts page shows how many loans you have, and their total when they share a currency.
  • In the loan's Details you can also rename it, change its logo, include or exclude it from the forecast, and archive it. Archiving keeps the loan's history; it disappears from forms and the forecast.

Note: A loan counts as money you owe, so it is part of what you owe in your net worth, which lives in Insights.
