Budget

Monthly limits, or let Damla suggest them from your history.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Step by step
  • How the suggestion is made
  • When a budget is in place
  • Good to know
  • Related

The budget sets a monthly limit for each spending category and shows how the current month is going against those limits. Damla suggests the limits from your past spending and cash flow, and you can change any figure before you accept it.

When to use it

  • You want a monthly figure per category, worked out from what you actually spent, not a number from a template.
  • You keep overspending in one or two categories and want a clear signal during the month, not at the end.
  • You want to know how much room your income leaves after your usual spending and the commitments Damla already knows about.
  • You want a savings target as a share of your income.

Step by step

  1. Open the Insights tab and tap Budget. If you have no budget yet, the screen says No budget yet and offers Suggest a budget. If you already have one, tap Ask for a fresh suggestion.
  2. On the Suggested budget screen, set Keep about … % of income with the slider. It starts at 10 percent, moves in steps of 5 and goes up to 40.
  3. Check the summary: Typical monthly income, Already promised monthly (instalments and staged purchase payments, as a monthly average; the line is hidden when nothing is promised) and Left after this budget.
  4. Read the notes under Worth knowing, if any.
  5. Change any limit by typing in the field beside its row.
  6. Tap Use this budget to accept, or Cancel to leave with nothing set.

Nothing is set until you accept a suggestion.

Note: Moving the slider clears any figures you have typed in, so set it first and then adjust the rows.

How the suggestion is made

Damla looks back at your complete months of recorded spending — up to six of them — and takes the middle of each category's monthly amounts as its usual figure. The suggestion starts there.

  • A category is a Steady cost when it has been present in at least three months since it first appeared and its amounts vary by no more than a fifth of their average. Steady costs are not trimmed.
  • Any other category is Flexible. Flexible categories are trimmed only as far as needed to reach the savings goal, and never by more than 35 percent each.
  • The savings goal is the share of income you set with the slider.
  • The footer says how many full months of your history the suggestion is based on.

If there is no history to work from, the screen says Not enough history yet and asks you to import a statement or record some spending first.

Note: With fewer than three full months of history, one of the notes says the suggestion is a rough guide. Adding earlier statements sharpens it.

When a budget is in place

Once you have accepted a budget, the Budget screen shows:

  • Left to spend this month: the total of all limits minus what you have spent so far this month, with a progress bar and the line "X of Y · day N of M".
  • By category: each limit with what you have spent so far. A red bar and "Over by …" mean you have passed the limit. An orange bar and "Spending faster than the month is passing" mean you are more than 15 percent ahead of the month's pace.

Under the progress are two buttons: Ask for a fresh suggestion and Remove this budget.

Good to know

  • The budget sets limits for spending categories in your main currency. It does not move money, create accounts or block anything.
  • Income for the suggestion is what actually came in over your history. If nothing was recorded, it falls back to the income you set up in the Plan tab, and a note says so.
  • Typing 0 in a limit field drops that category from the budget you accept. Emptying the field does not: it falls back to the suggested figure.
  • Notes under Worth knowing point out a forecast dip below your minimum balance, a category that was unusually expensive last month, or a shortfall no amount of trimming can close. If your history shows you spending more than you earn, a note names the monthly gap.
  • The budget reports what you have spent against what you set. It is not financial advice.
