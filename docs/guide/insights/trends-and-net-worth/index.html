Trends and net worth

Net worth today and over time, spending month against month, the monthly review.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Net worth
  • Trends
  • Good to know
  • Related

Insights has two screens for the long view. Net worth shows everything you own minus everything you owe, today, in your main currency. Trends draws net worth over time, month against month of spending, and a review of one month.

When to use it

  • You want one number for what you are worth, with the pieces that make it up.
  • You want to see whether that number is moving up or down across the months.
  • You want to compare this month's spending with last month, category by category.
  • At the end of a month, you want to look back at what came in, what went out and what was saved.

Net worth

Open the Insights tab and tap Net worth. The row on the Insights screen already shows the figure, with what you own and what you owe.

  • The top card shows You are worth, with You own and You owe beside it.
  • Below it is the row See it over time, which opens Trends on its Net worth tab.
  • If you still owe money on staged purchases, they are listed under Promised, not yet paid, each with its due date. A line After these commitments follows them, showing what net worth would be once they are paid. They are not counted as debts in the top figure.
  • The Everything section lists your accounts, holdings, assets and debts, each with its amount in your main currency, or in its own currency when there is no rate for it yet. A holding shows its valuation as a number of units times a unit value, with the as-of date. It shows · out of date when the value is stale.

Net worth counts investments and other assets at the value you gave them, not only at what you paid. An item that needs a rate you have not set is flagged "Needs an exchange rate for" with the currency named. Something unlikely to be repaid is left out, with the line "Left out: unlikely to be repaid".

Note: Net worth lives in Insights, not on Accounts. The Accounts screen shows only the cash you can spend; net worth counts everything, including things you could not pay a bill with.

Trends

Tap Trends on the Insights screen — it opens on the Monthly review tab. Pick one of three tabs at the top.

Net worth

A chart of net worth at the end of each month, ending with a point for today. It is drawn from your recorded transactions and the values you gave holdings, so it moves when you record something or update a value, and not by itself. A card on top gives the latest figure and how much it has changed since the earliest point, with a date. With fewer than two month ends to show, the screen says Not enough history yet.

Spending

A bar chart of spending by month, with the five biggest categories over those months coloured in and the rest gathered as Other. Up to six months are shown side by side. The last month is the month so far, so its figures cover only the days that have passed. Underneath, This month against last lists up to eight of the biggest categories with what changed: an arrow and an amount, and a percentage where it can be worked out. A category with nothing last month says New this month. Changes carry both a sign arrow and a colour, so you can read the direction either way.

Monthly review

The review of one month — the last complete one first, or any of the months in the menu at the top. Each part appears only when there is something to show:

  • A Saved card, or Saved so far for the current month: what came in minus what was spent, and as a share of what came in when anything came in.
  • Came in, Spent and, when there is an earlier month, Last month spent beside it.
  • Where it went: the biggest categories of the month with their change against the month before.
  • Biggest expenses: the three largest single payments of the month, with dates.
  • Net worth over the month: how your net worth moved while it lasted.

Good to know

  • All the figures are in your main currency, converted with the rates on file. An amount with no rate is left out of the totals until you set one.
  • Trends shows at most six months of month-by-month spending; the net worth chart reaches further back, one point per month end.
  • The biggest-expenses list counts confirmed entries only, and leaves out reconciliation adjustments, so a correction made only to match a statement does not crowd out real spending.
  • These screens give information about your own history. They are estimates and records, not financial advice.
