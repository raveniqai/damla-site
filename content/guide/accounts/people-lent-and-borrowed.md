# People: lent and borrowed

People keeps a balance with each person you lend money to or borrow from. Lending is not spending and being repaid is not income: the money moves between your account and the person, so your spending and income figures are not affected.

![People: money lent and borrowed.](damla-people.png)

## When to use it

- You lent someone money and want to remember who owes whom, and how much.
- You borrowed from a family member or a friend and want to see what you still owe.
- You want repayments kept out of your spending and income figures, while the money still moves in and out of your accounts.

## Step by step

1. Open Plan, then People: lent and borrowed, and tap **Add**.
2. Type the person's name in the box headed *New person* and tap **Add**.
3. Open the person from the list.
4. Under **Record**, choose what happened: **I lent**, **I borrowed**, **They paid me back** or **I paid them back**.
5. Enter the amount, the account the money moved through and the date, then tap **Save**.

The person's balance shows who owes whom: positive means they owe you, negative means you owe them. When nothing is owed either way, it says **All settled**.

## Good to know

- You can also add a person from Accounts: tap the plus button, then *Add a person*.
- People also appear on the Accounts page, under People, each with who owes whom and by how much.
- Lending or being repaid is never counted as spending or income. The money moves between your account and the person, and nowhere else.
- A person can have a balance in more than one currency. Each balance is kept in its own currency; the list shows a line for each.
- To add a debt that already exists when you start, use the set-up guide. In the Loans and debts step, tap **Add**, then *Money with a person*.
- If a balance will not be repaid, open the person and use the button at the bottom of the page: write off money owed to you that will not be repaid, or mark what you owe as forgiven.
- A repayment you record moves real money on the day you enter it. It is in your account balances from that day, and the forecast starts from those balances.

> **Note:** The People list is sorted by name, and each row shows the balance at a glance. At the top of a person's page the balance is written out in full — *Sam owes you*, or *You owe Sam*.

> **Careful:** This page is for money between people. A loan from a bank, paid back in instalments, belongs under Loans instead.
