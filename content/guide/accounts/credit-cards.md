# Credit cards

Damla records a card purchase on the day it happens, and the cash leaves your bank on the day you pay the bill. You see both where the money goes and when it goes.

![A credit card: what you owe and the next bill.](damla-card.png)

## When to use it

- To add a card and set its billing cycle.
- To see what you owe now, what the next bill will be and when it is due.
- To tell Damla what your last statement says is still unpaid.
- To spread a large purchase over instalments.

## Step by step

1. Add the card: on Accounts tap the plus button and choose *Add an account*, pick the card type and enter what you owe today.
2. Open the card from Accounts, tap the card to flip it and tap **Details**.
3. Under **Billing cycle**, choose **Paid from** — the bank account, savings or wallet that pays the bill. Without a paying account, the forecast cannot see the card bill.
4. Set **Statement date** and **Payment due date** with the steppers, each as a day of the month. The footer shows the next statement and payment dates so you can check them against your bank's app.
5. Once Damla has a last statement to show, **Details** gains a **Last statement** section. Enter the amount your bank says is still unpaid in *Still to pay* and tap **Update the last statement**. Damla then pays that on its due date, and the rest of what you owe comes with the next statement. Enter 0 once you have paid it.
6. Optionally, fill in **Typical month of spending**: what the card usually spends in a statement. The forecast uses it for statements that have not been issued yet.

## Good to know

> **Note:** A card purchase is an expense on the day it happens, with a category, and feeds your spending reports. Paying the statement is a transfer from your bank to the card, so the money is not counted as spending a second time. The forecast takes the cash out of your bank account on the card's due date, not on the purchase date.

- The card opens in Accounts saying *You owe*. When Damla can see a bill coming, the card shows the next bill and its date; until then it shows what you spent in the last 30 days. The back of the card shows the statement date and due date with the next date of each, the next bill with its amount, the account it is paid from and what was spent in the last 30 days.
- The *Still to pay* figure matters. Without it, everything you owe counts as the next statement, a month away. With it, the total you owe does not change — only when it will leave.
- **Typical month of spending** is a starting figure only. A statement that has been issued always keeps its real amount, and once the card has as many complete months in Damla as the Expected card spending setting averages, the average of those months replaces it. Leave it empty to use the card's own history from the start. That history needs two complete months; until then, the amount of the last statement is used.
- You can also enter the *Still to pay* amount when you add the card.
- Once Damla knows the card's last digits, bank alerts add their purchases by themselves. An alert whose digits match no account yet waits in the Inbox; complete it once and Damla learns the digits.
- To spread a purchase over months, swipe it in Activity and tap **Turn into instalments**, or say so in a sentence. Type the numbers from your bank's offer — the instalment, the months, any fee — and tap **Convert**. With a sentence, Damla shows the plan it read before creating it. Nothing changes until you tap the button. Damla does not work out interest.

> **Note:** A purchase in another currency is recorded in the card's own currency as an estimate, worked out from the latest exchange rate and the markup you set under Settings, Card purchases in another currency (2.5% when you leave it empty). It shows with an ≈ mark and the amount the merchant charged. When the statement is imported, the estimate is replaced by what the bank really billed. If Damla has no rate for that currency yet, the alert waits in the Inbox and asks what the card was billed; it is never guessed.
