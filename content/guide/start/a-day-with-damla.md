# A day with Damla

Today is the screen Damla shows you every day: one big answer on top - what is safe to spend - and everything that needs you underneath. This page is the daily habit.

![Today with the sample household.](damla-today.png)

## When to use it

- In the morning, to see what today looks like before anything leaves your account.
- When something is due, to record it in a tap from the Due list.
- When a bank message went somewhere unexpected, to find it waiting for your OK.
- When you want to know what the money went to this month.

## Step by step

1. Open **Today**. The big card shows **Safe to spend** and a verdict: either it *stays above your minimum* for the period, or it says *You may run short* before a date. Tap the card to open the **Forecast** and see day by day where the low point is.
2. Look at the three numbers under the card: **Balance today**, the **Lowest point** in the period with its date, and **You keep**, your minimum.
3. Under the numbers, anything that needs an answer appears as a row, in this order:
   - **Due** (income and bills due tomorrow, today or already late, not recorded yet).
   - Statements waiting to review (sent by the Shortcut, nothing added until you confirm).
   - Items waiting for your OK (the Inbox).
   - Bills found in your history (repeating payments Damla noticed; add them in one tap).
   - Bills coming (announced by a message but not charged yet; counted once the card is charged).
   - Finish setting up (steps you left for later).
   - A backup reminder, when the automatic backup has not run for a few days, or is switched off while your books already have entries.
4. Tap the row that needs you. A due payment opens its page to record it; a card bill opens Pay card bill (amount, account, date); a staged purchase opens its plan. Alerts that were clear enough to record go straight into the Activity list, under **New from alerts**.
5. Scan **Coming up** underneath: money in and money out together, each with its date, and tappable rows that go straight to where it is recorded. If one of your bank accounts has a *Keep at least* amount and it would dip below that before the month ends, a **Move … to …** row appears with how much to move - tap it to see the working.
6. Read the **Where the money went** row for how much you spent this month, and **Ask Damla** if you have a question about something you plan to do.

> **Tip:** If you have set money aside for goals, a line on the teal card says how much of safe to spend is already spoken for and how much is free to spend. Safe to spend itself does not change.

If you see You may run short, it means the lowest point of your balance in the period falls below your minimum before the end of it. The Forecast shows when. What if (a button in the Forecast) lets you try spending, receiving, skipping a payment or turning something into instalments before you do it for real.

## Good to know

> **Note:** The number is only as good as the things Damla knows. Today's answer is worked out from your books as they are now: when you add an account, a bill or a card change, the answer is that new calculation - you never have to refresh anything yourself.

> **Limit:** Everyday spending that is not a bill and comes from your bank accounts is not taken out of the balance day by day in the forecast. That is what your minimum is for: keep a cushion, not an exact figure, for the ordinary days.

> **Note:** In the last three days of the free trial a one-line banner about the trial appears near the top of Today, with a Subscribe button. Today's answer always comes first.

Small habits that keep the answer right:

- Record things when they happen - **Type a sentence** in the Add menu, or let your bank messages reach Damla through a Shortcut so it happens by itself.
- Open the Inbox and clear what is waiting for your OK, rather than leaving it.
- When something repeats that is not a bill yet, add it from the bills found in your history row so the forecast counts it.
- Keep the backup reminder clear: everything lives on the phone, so the backup is your copy.
- When the answer says short, look early: open the Forecast.
